 Comments 

- This paper lies within the field of combinatorial optimization and metaheuristics,
 more specifically at the intersection of Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS/GVNS) and reinforcement learning (RL) 
 for adaptive operator selection. 

- This work proposes an RL-enhanced GVNS framework TSP integrating Q-learning both in initialization and local search phase.
 It addresses the limitation of fixed operator ordering in classical GVNS by introducing adaptive mechanisms: 
 RL-driven VNS using Q-learning + MARL-based initialization + adaptive shaking strategy

- The paper is in line with recent trends in ML for metaheuristics. The methodology is clear. The paper is  well structured. 

- The decomposition into initialization, shaking, and local search phases is standard. 

- The operator-to-operator transitions is simple but computationally efficient. 

What i need to do: 

- However, it is defined only as the previously applied operator which is a very limited representation of the search context. 
  This choice should be justified or faced as limitation. 
  
- The integration of MARL and RL-GVNS is presented, but the interaction between these components is not described in detail.

- What is the rationale for reward function? And the asymmetry between positive and negative rewards and the fixed penalty term?

- Q-table is reinitialized for each local search call which limits long-term learning and contradicts the idea of accumulating knowledge across the search.
Limitation?

- The instances are mostly small to medium scale; only one large instance (pcb442) is included. 

- The improvements are relatively small in several cases. Limitation?

- The parameter tuning process is described informally. systematic calibration is needed. 

- Minor: Some sentences are repetitive, particularly in the introduction. 

- Some references are incomplete (authors missing!). 

- Old references, recent works on RL for combinatorial optimization and neural heuristics are missing

convergence behavior for small instances

(-) The contribution is incremental. 
learning design is simple. choices are not sufficiently justified.
